# Interface direction

## Folded knowledge sheet

The workspace is a calm paper-like surface where research is arranged spatially. A cluster is a sheet within the sheet: open clusters frame related records; folded clusters preserve a small index of what they contain. Relationship curves read as creases that explain how ideas connect.

The direction keeps the original brief's light canvas, quiet grid, restrained semantic colors, thin borders, compact controls, and generous whitespace. The graph is the main surface; the inspector and focused views provide detail without turning the canvas into a dashboard of cards.

## Visual language

- **Canvas:** warm near-white, fine low-contrast dot grid, subtle paper boundary.
- **Knowledge records:** white cards with one semantic mark and a short type label. Claims carry evidence status; sources expose their domain; AI proposals are labeled.
- **Clusters:** pale green-tinted grouping sheets with fold/unfold control and a compact folded preview.
- **Relationships:** quiet curved lines with short labels; direction remains visible without competing with record titles.
- **Controls:** small, consistently sized buttons with visible keyboard focus and descriptive accessible names.
- **Motion:** brief state transitions only; reduced-motion preferences are honored.

## Interaction contract

- Drag records to arrange the sheet; use the spacebar or hand tool to pan and the wheel to zoom.
- Select two records with the relationship tool to connect them.
- Fold a cluster to temporarily hide its contained records.
- Add and edit records in place; use the inspector for metadata and claim status.
- AI can read the current graph and prepare changes, but review decisions are required before graph updates are committed.
- Search, outline, evidence, source, question, and history views are projections of the same persistent graph.

## Type and color

Native system sans-serif keeps interface text crisp across platforms; Georgia headings give the research sheet an editorial tone. Semantic colors are limited to muted green for supported knowledge, terracotta for claims and questions needing attention, blue for conceptual material, and neutral gray for structure. Color is paired with text labels so state is not conveyed by hue alone.
